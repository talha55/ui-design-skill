# Art direction

A page people remember has one strong idea carried all the way through. A page people forget has twelve reasonable decisions and no idea. Decide the idea first.

Left alone, you will make safe choices: a tidy hero, a card grid, a familiar sans-serif, a gentle fade. Every one of those is defensible and the sum is forgettable. This file exists to stop that. Be bolder than feels comfortable, then check it against the business.

## The big idea

Write one sentence that could only describe this site: what a visitor sees and feels in the first five seconds.

Find it in the business itself:

- **Its material:** water, flame, flour, ink, timber, steel, cloth, light.
- **Its moment:** the relief when the problem is fixed, the first bite, the reveal of a finished room.
- **Its place:** a harbour town, a city lane, a workshop, a kitchen at 5 am.
- **Its attitude:** blunt, generous, precise, mischievous, calm.

Then turn it into something visible: a headline set so large it becomes the image, a photograph that fills the screen and opens like a door, a colour nobody else in the trade would dare, a scroll sequence that tells the story of one job from start to finish.

If the sentence could describe a competitor's site, it is not an idea yet.

## Four signatures

Choose the one that suits the business. Mix elements only when you can say why.

### Cinematic scroll

The page is a sequence of scenes. Full-screen photographs or video, sections that hold still while the content inside them moves, a story told in order.

- **Layout:** full-bleed scenes, one idea per screen, generous black or white space around text.
- **Type:** large, calm headings; short lines of text that appear one at a time.
- **Colour:** taken from the photography. Dark or very light backgrounds so images carry the colour.
- **Signature moves:** a pinned scene where an image scales from a framed picture to the full screen; text that changes as the scene progresses; a horizontal gallery driven by vertical scroll; slow parallax on large images.
- **Suits:** premium services, hospitality, property, architecture, products with a story, anything with strong photography.

### Bold editorial

The page is a magazine spread. Type is the main visual. The grid is strong and then deliberately broken.

- **Layout:** asymmetric columns, oversized headings that run edge to edge, images cropped hard and placed off-centre, numbers and labels as design elements, thin rules.
- **Type:** a characterful display face at extreme sizes against a quiet text face. Big contrast in weight and size: a heading three to six times the size of the text beside it.
- **Colour:** paper and ink plus one strong colour, used in blocks.
- **Signature moves:** headlines that rise line by line from behind a mask; images revealed by a wipe; a section whose background colour changes as it arrives; sharp, quick motion with no bounce.
- **Suits:** professional practices, studios, fashion, food with a point of view, trades that want to look established and confident.

### Dark and vivid

A dark stage with one electric colour. Depth, glow and precision.

- **Layout:** dark surfaces at slightly different depths, fine borders, tight spacing, content arranged in panels of different sizes.
- **Type:** a sharp sans-serif, sometimes paired with a monospace for labels and numbers.
- **Colour:** near-black tinted towards the accent hue, one saturated accent, text in off-white. The accent glows: soft light behind key elements, gradients that move slowly, a fine grain over the whole page.
- **Signature moves:** a hero background that is alive (a slow gradient, a field of light that follows the pointer); cards that tilt towards the cursor; borders that light up on hover; numbers and labels that tick into place.
- **Suits:** technology, night-time businesses, automotive, fitness, music, modern trades that want to look sharp.

### Playful and kinetic

Things move, and the page is fun to touch.

- **Layout:** colour blocks, rounded shapes, stickers and labels at angles, elements that overlap.
- **Type:** a rounded or chunky display face, set large, sometimes rotated or on a curve.
- **Colour:** two or three bright colours at full strength with a warm neutral.
- **Signature moves:** marquee bands of text; buttons that pull towards the cursor; hovers with spring and overshoot; elements that rotate or wobble as they enter; a custom cursor that reacts to what it is over.
- **Suits:** food and drink, events, children's services, creative studios, shops, any brand with a sense of humour.

## Make it bold

Whichever signature you choose:

- **One thing is huge.** A headline at 10 to 16 percent of the viewport width, or an image that fills the screen. Scale is the cheapest way to look confident.
- **Contrast is extreme.** Very large beside very small, very heavy beside very light, a full-colour block beside empty space. Medium everything reads as a template.
- **The first screen is a statement.** Not a headline, a paragraph and two buttons in the middle of a plain background.
- **Backgrounds have atmosphere.** A photograph, a colour field, a gradient with grain, a texture, a large cropped letterform. Flat white or flat grey from top to bottom is a missed chance.
- **Every section has a different shape.** If two sections in a row could swap places unnoticed, redesign one.
- **There are three or more moments.** Places where something happens that the visitor did not expect: the opening sequence, a scene in the middle of the page, the way the page ends. Name them before building.
- **Typefaces are chosen.** Never fall back to the fonts every generated page uses: no Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans or system defaults for headings. Look for a display face with real character.

## Keep it honest and usable

Bold is not the same as careless. These still hold:

- The business's supplied brand comes first. A supplied logo, colour or typeface is the starting point of the idea, not an obstacle to it.
- The main action is visible and usable at once, on a phone, without waiting for an animation.
- Text can always be read: contrast passes, lines are a sensible length, nothing important is only shown in motion.
- Nothing is invented about the business.
- Someone who has asked their device for reduced motion gets a calm, complete page.

## When to hold back

Use the quiet level instead when the request says simple, minimal, conservative or plain, when an existing brand is itself restrained, or when the visitor is likely to be anxious or in a hurry and theatre would be in the way. In those cases keep one moment (usually the opening) and make the rest calm.
