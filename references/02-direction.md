# Step 2: Direction checkpoint

Before building, show the person what you intend to build, in a form they can judge in half a minute. Changing a paragraph is cheap. Rebuilding a page is not.

## The direction summary

Keep it short. Use exactly these parts:

- **The idea:** the one-sentence big idea, and which signature it uses (cinematic scroll, bold editorial, dark and vivid, or playful and kinetic).
- **Style:** one sentence in plain words.
- **Palette:** each colour with its hex value and its role. Give the role in a few words, such as "headings and footer" or "primary button only".
- **Type:** the heading face and the text face, and where they come from.
- **Sections:** the ordered list of sections, one line each, saying what each one is for.
- **Photos:** the mood in one line: the subject matter, the light, and what to avoid.
- **Moments:** the three or more moments the page is built around, one line each: the opening, the centrepiece, the ending.
- **Motion:** full, medium or quiet, as defined in `references/07-motion.md`. Full is the default.
- **Left out on purpose:** anything a visitor might expect that you are not including because it was not supplied, such as reviews.

End with one question: **"Build this, or change something?"**

Then stop.

## What counts as a yes

"Yes", "go", "build it", "looks good", or an answer that only adds detail. Anything that changes a part ("warmer colours", "no video") is a change: update that part of the summary, show the changed part only, and confirm in one line before building. Do not restart the whole summary.

If the answer is unclear, ask one specific question. Do not ask several.

## When the checkpoint is skipped

See the checkpoint rule in `SKILL.md`. When you skip the wait, the summary still exists: put it in one short paragraph at the top of your reply so the person can see what was decided on their behalf.

## Do not

- Do not offer a menu of five styles. Make a decision and show it. Offer an alternative only if the brief genuinely points two ways, and then offer exactly two.
- Do not pad the summary with reasoning. The person wants to see the direction, not the argument for it.
- Do not start writing code while waiting.
