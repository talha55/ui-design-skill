# Step 8: Final check

Before you hand the page over, read it as a stranger would. Go through both lists. Fix what you find; do not just note it.

## List one: does it look machine-made?

Each line is something to look for and remove.

- A purple, violet or blue-to-purple gradient that the brand did not ask for.
- A centred headline over a centred paragraph over two centred buttons, followed by three identical cards with an icon in a circle.
- Every section centred, with the same padding and the same shape as the one before.
- Emoji used as icons or as bullets.
- Icons on everything, including things that needed no icon.
- Glass panels, glows, blurred blobs or gradient text with no reason to be there.
- A row of badges, counters or logos under the headline.
- Copy that could describe any business: "quality you can trust", "your satisfaction is our priority", "we go above and beyond".
- More than two typefaces, or a default system font where a chosen face was planned.
- Everything rounded to the same large radius, so nothing has hierarchy.
- Stock photos that do not belong together, or a person presented as staff.
- Sections that exist because pages usually have them: an empty "Why choose us" with four adjectives, a "Our process" with steps nobody needed explained.
- Uniform, even weight everywhere, so the eye has nowhere to land first.

Then ask one question: if the business name were removed, could a visitor tell what kind of business this is and where, from the first screen alone? If not, the hero is too generic.

## List two: is everything true?

Check every sentence, number, name and image against what was supplied.

None of these may appear unless the business provided them:

- Reviews, testimonials, star ratings, review counts.
- Statistics: jobs completed, customers served, response times, percentages.
- Years in business, founding dates, "family owned", "locally owned".
- Awards, accreditations, memberships, licence or registration numbers.
- Client or partner logos.
- Team members, names, roles, photos presented as the team.
- Prices, discounts, "free quote", "no call-out fee".
- Guarantees, warranties, "24/7", "same day", "fully insured".
- Suburb or street names, a street address, a map location.
- Social media links.

Watch for the quiet versions: "trusted by locals", "hundreds of happy customers", "Brackenford's favourite". These are claims too.

Where the page would be stronger with one of these, put a clearly labelled placeholder in its place, styled so it cannot be mistaken for real content, or leave the section out. Then add it to the handover list.

Also check:

- Every media URL was fetched and loaded during step 5. Any that were not are placeholders.
- Contact details match the brief exactly, character for character.
- Stock photos carry no caption that implies they show the business.

## Technical check

- No sideways scroll at 390 px.
- Every interactive element has visible hover and focus states.
- Reduced motion is respected.
- Icons are one family.
- The page has a title, a description, a language attribute and one `h1`.

## The handover list

End your reply with this, filled in:

**Still needed from you**

- Each placeholder on the page and what should replace it.
- Facts the page would be stronger with, if they are true: reviews, licence numbers, years in business, service suburbs, pricing.
- The business's own photos, and a logo if there is none.
- Anything you assumed, stated plainly, so it can be corrected.
